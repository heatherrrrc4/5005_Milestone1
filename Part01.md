## the dataset I collected is my Youtube watch history, exported as a JSON file. It contains structured information such as titles of videos, timestamps, URLs, and even where I get the content(whether the video came from ads or recommendations). At first glance, the data appears highly organized and precise, presenting my viewing behavior as a clear timeline of actions.

## However, upon closer inspection, the dataset reveals more than just what I watched—it begins to suggest patterns and assumptions about me. For example, the presence of ambient music videos for sleep, entertainment content, and even advertisement-related entries indicates how my attention shifts across different emotional and functional needs. This reflects a predictive layer, where the platform is not only recording my behavior but also shaping what I am likely to watch next.

## At the same time, the data also exposes a limitation. While it records every click in detail, it does not capture why I chose those videos or how I felt at the moment. The dataset becomes an incomplete archive. Sometimes I misclicked the video or I just use the content for my assignment(eg. background music for my project) .

## Ultimately, this dataset reflects both personal habits and broader digital culture. It shows how individual behavior is intertwined with algorithmic systems, where personal choice and platform influence become difficult to separate.
